# Customer Churn & Retention Intelligence

A SQL-only customer analytics project built using the **Olist Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset** to understand customer purchasing behaviour, identify inactive customer segments, analyse historical revenue exposure, and develop data-driven retention strategies.

## 1. Project Overview

Customer Churn & Retention Intelligence analyses e-commerce transaction data using PostgreSQL.

The project covers customer purchase behaviour, repeat purchasing patterns, cohort analysis, RFM segmentation, customer lifetime revenue, inactivity analysis, revenue concentration, and retention prioritisation.

The objective is to convert raw transactional data into meaningful business insights that can support customer retention decisions.

## 2. Business Problem

An e-commerce business wants to understand:

- Which customers have become inactive?
- How many customers make only one purchase?
- Which customer segments contribute the most revenue?
- How much historical revenue is associated with inactive customers?
- Which high-value inactive customers should receive retention attention?
- How can future retention campaigns be measured effectively?

## 3. Business Objectives

- Design and validate a relational database using PostgreSQL.
- Analyse customer purchasing behaviour and repeat purchase patterns.
- Perform customer cohort and RFM analysis.
- Calculate customer lifetime revenue and revenue concentration.
- Identify inactive and high-value inactive customer segments.
- Develop retention priorities and proposed business actions.
- Design a treatment-versus-control measurement framework.
- Analyse SQL execution plans and test query performance optimisations.
- Consolidate key findings into an executive KPI summary.

## 4. Dataset Description

**Dataset:** Olist Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset  
**Source:** Kaggle

The dataset contains nine CSV files covering:

- Customers
- Orders
- Order items
- Order payments
- Order reviews
- Products
- Sellers
- Geolocation
- Product category translations

The analysis primarily uses delivered orders and product prices for customer revenue calculations.

**Revenue scope:** Product revenue is calculated using `order_items.price`. Freight charges are excluded.

## 8. Analytical Methodology

### Database Design and Validation

Created nine relational tables, imported source data, and validated record counts, uniqueness, null values, and relationships.

### Data Quality and Cleaning

Audited customer, order, payment, and product records. Identified missing information, invalid values, timestamp anomalies, and records requiring review. Preserved source records and documented quality issues.

### Customer Behaviour Analysis

Analysed customer order frequency, one-time purchases, repeat purchases, and the time gap between first and second purchases.

### Cohort Analysis

Grouped customers by their first purchase month and examined repeat purchasing behaviour across subsequent months.

### RFM Segmentation

Applied Recency, Frequency, and Monetary scoring to identify customer segments such as Champions, Loyal Customers, Potential Loyalists, At Risk, and Hibernating customers.

### Customer Lifetime Revenue

Calculated historical product revenue at the real-customer level and analysed revenue distribution across customer quartiles.

### Revenue Concentration

Measured how much historical revenue was generated by the highest-value customer groups.

### Customer Inactivity Analysis

Classified customers using project-defined inactivity thresholds and identified high-value inactive segments for retention prioritisation.

### Retention Strategy

Mapped customer priority groups to proposed retention actions and measurable campaign KPIs.

### Experiment Measurement Framework

Designed a proposed treatment-versus-control allocation and defined incremental purchase lift calculations.

### Query Performance Optimization

Used `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` to inspect execution plans, tested a customer lookup index, and compared query performance under different session-level `work_mem` settings.

## 11. Revenue Exposure Analysis

Customers inactive for more than 180 days:

- **65,963 customers**
- **70.66% of delivered-order customers**
- **R$9,261,584.55 historical product revenue associated with these customers**
- **70.05% of total historical product revenue**

High-value customers inactive for more than 90 days:

- **21,049 customers**
- **R$7,455,836.07 historical product revenue**
- **56.39% of total historical product revenue**

These figures represent historical revenue associated with inactive customers. They are not predictions of future lost revenue or verified financial losses.

## 12. Retention Strategy

The project defines six retention priority groups:

| Priority Group | Proposed Strategy |
|---|---|
| Critical Priority | Personalised win-back strategy |
| High Priority | Targeted retention offer test |
| Medium Priority | Customer engagement campaign |
| Monitor High-Value | Loyalty and repeat-purchase engagement |
| Low Value - Long Inactive | Low-cost automated re-engagement |
| Standard Monitoring | Regular customer activity monitoring |

The project also defines campaign KPIs, treatment/control allocation, and incremental lift measurement requirements.

## 13. Query Performance Optimization Findings

Performance experiments were conducted using PostgreSQL execution plans and observed execution times.

### Customer Lookup Index

A candidate index on `olist.orders(customer_id)` was tested for a selective customer lookup.

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Execution time | 11.624 ms | 0.161 ms |

Observed improvement: **98.61%**

The execution plan changed from a Sequential Scan to an Index Scan for this selective lookup.

### Session-Level Memory Optimization

The `work_mem` setting was tested at 4MB and 16MB for two analytical queries.

| Query | 4MB | 16MB | Improvement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Q2 Revenue Aggregation | 2,355.693 ms | 1,930.233 ms | 18.06% |
| Q3 Customer Purchase History | 2,143.999 ms | 1,633.305 ms | 23.82% |

The execution plans changed from External Merge sorting to Quicksort for both queries.

The original `work_mem` setting of 4MB was restored after testing.

**Note:** These are observed execution times from individual tests. Results can vary with cache state and system workload. The 16MB setting was tested on two queries and is not a universal production recommendation.

## 14. Executive Business Recommendations

1. Prioritise high-value inactive customers for retention investigation.
2. Design personalised win-back experiments for critical-priority customers.
3. Monitor customers inactive for more than 180 days.
4. Track revenue concentration among high-value customers.

## 15. Project Limitations

- The analysis uses historical Olist transaction data.
- Inactivity thresholds are project-defined and do not represent a verified business churn definition.
- No machine-learning churn prediction model has been implemented.
- Historical revenue exposure is not equivalent to predicted future revenue loss.
- Actual campaign exposure, cost, and response data are unavailable.
- Treatment/control allocation is a planning simulation, not an executed experiment.
- Campaign ROI and actual incremental lift cannot be calculated without campaign outcome data.
- Performance results are based on controlled SQL experiments and may vary under different workloads.
- The tested index and `work_mem` settings have not been validated in a production environment.

## 16. How to Run the Project

1. Download the Olist Brazilian E-Commerce dataset from Kaggle.
2. Install PostgreSQL 16+ and DBeaver Community.
3. Create a database named `churn_project`.
4. Create the `olist` schema.
5. Import the nine CSV files into their corresponding PostgreSQL tables.
6. Execute SQL scripts in the numbered project folders.
7. Review the validation queries and analytical outputs.
8. Run the optimization scripts to reproduce the documented performance experiments.

Refer to individual SQL files for table definitions, data validation, analysis, optimization experiments, and business recommendations.
